02 · THE IDEA

The idea and the problem

PROBLEM

Panels lose their light

In desert, coastal and industrial settings, dust, salt and soot build up on solar panels, block part of the sunlight and cut energy output. Engineers know the problem well, but it is invisible to most people.

IDEA

Step inside the cell

Instead of a lecture, the player dives into a silicon cell and meets the contaminants as tangible things: dust clouds, salt crystals and carbon spheres. The mission is to get photons to the receivers.

LEARNING

Science you discover by playing

Each wavelength has a job: infrared vaporizes dust, ultraviolet shatters salt, and visible light charges the receivers. Players learn that light carries different energies without reading a single lesson.

The goal is for players to walk away understanding why panels get cleaned, why the light that reaches them matters, and how those details become the energy that lights a city.

The finale ties the experience to Saudi Vision 2030: a solar plant at full capacity powering a city of the future. The figures shown in that scene are illustrative storytelling; the panel calculator is where the real, sourced estimates live.

04 · GAMEPLAY

Gameplay

Goal: get the beam to every receiver in the cell as visible light. Click or tap a prism to turn it 90°, and switch wavelength from the top bar or with keys 1 · 2 · 3.

InfraredIR · 850nm

Vaporizes soft dust clouds with thermal energy.

VisibleVIS · 500nm

The only band that charges and locks receivers.

UltravioletUV · 365nm

Shatters salt crystals; its photons carry more energy than their bonds.

ElementWhat it does
EmitterSource of the beam; its color follows the selected band.
PrismReflects the beam at a right angle; the player rotates it.
Dust cloudBlocks the beam; infrared vaporizes it.
Salt crystalBlocks the beam; ultraviolet shatters it into shards.
ReceiverLocks after about 1.5 seconds of visible light.
Energy ringConverts a passing beam to its own wavelength.
Magnetic vortexBends the beam 90° clockwise.
Spectral gateLets only its own wavelength through.
Carbon sphereDrifts back and forth, blocking the path at times.
WallStops the beam.
STARS

Up to 3 stars per cell: one for clearing it, one for time, one for rotations. The clock starts on the first move, and the targets are always visible in the status card.

06 · LUMEN A.I. & PANEL CARE

From playing to acting

CONTEXT TIPS

Lumen reads the board

  • Wrong band: if the beam stays on dust, a crystal or a receiver with the wrong light, Lumen explains why and the right button pulses.
  • Lost beam: if the beam leaves the board and the player is idle for 7 seconds, Lumen suggests rotating a prism.
  • First encounters: rings, vortices and carbon spheres are explained the first time they appear.
SOLVER HINT

The hint button

A breadth-first search over every prism and wavelength combination finds the shortest win from the current board, then reveals a single step: a glowing prism, a pulsing band button, or "hold the beam steady". A hint caps the run at two stars. Tested by solving all three cells with hints alone.

PANEL CARE CALCULATOR

Apply it to a real panel

From the main menu or the finale, players pick an environment that matches a game cell (desert dust, coastal salt or urban soot), a system size and the days since the last cleaning. The calculator estimates the current loss, the energy and grid CO₂ lost, what cleaning today saves over 30 days, and when to clean next, with safe, low-water cleaning tips.

08 · VISUAL DIRECTION

Visual direction

Materials and forms

  • Procedural textures generated in code: solar cells, city windows, silicon hexagons.
  • Refractive glass for prisms and crystals, sharing a single refraction buffer.
  • A reflective floor that mirrors the neon and adds depth.
  • A volumetric beam with a white-hot core, two aura layers and photon packets that carry light.

Lighting and post-processing

  • Volumetric light shafts and real god rays in the opening.
  • ACES filmic tone mapping with a grade of teal shadows and warm highlights.
  • Selective bloom, depth of field that follows the camera, and film grain.
  • Anamorphic lens streaks at the sun and at beam impact points.
The desert cell: dust clouds and metal-framed prisms.
The loading screen while shaders compile.

The world recovers in front of you

Fog color and density, the floor, the energy conduits, the music and the height of the city in the background are all tied to how much of the cell the player has cleaned, so the effect of their work shows everywhere.

10 · QUALITY

Quality, testing and performance

What was actually tested

  • An automated checker proves all three cells are solvable.
  • All three cells solved using hints alone, with every suggestion correct.
  • A full player play-through: odd names, pausing, retrying, switching language, deleting profiles.
  • Phone emulation with touch, and 1080p on an integrated GPU.
  • 11 passing checks for the panel calculator.
  • A final code review: 8 findings, all fixed and verified.

Fixing the black-screen flicker

Two causes were found by measurement and fixed: a GPU memory leak in the shadow component, and every shader recompiling whenever an obstacle was destroyed.

Final code review: 8 findings fixed

  • Enter on a focused button no longer starts the game by mistake.
  • Corrupted save data is repaired instead of crashing the menu.
  • A hint that suggests no move no longer costs a star.
  • Wavelength tips account for rings that recolor the beam.
  • The refraction buffer is lighter in Performance mode.
  • The city is no longer recomputed every frame for nothing.
  • Audio no longer schedules redundant changes.
  • A clear screen appears if the browser blocks 3D graphics.
